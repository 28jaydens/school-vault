Glycolysis and Cellular Respiration

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
Cellular respiration is how both autotrophs and heterotrophs get usable energy (ATP) back out of glucose. Glycolysis is its first stage: one glucose is split in half to collect a little ATP and some electrons.

[!info] Scope
Glycolysis is not on the Test 2 outline, but it is in my notebook and the outline says more may be added. Know the overview and how it links to photosynthesis; the step-by-step detail is probably for the next unit.

Key Concepts

Cellular respiration (overall)

$$C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6\,O_2 \rightarrow 6\,CO_2 + 6\,H_2O + \text{ATP (+ heat)}$$

It is the reverse of the photosynthesis equation.

Photosynthesis Cellular respiration
Energy Stores it (light → glucose) Releases it (glucose → ATP)
Reactants CO₂ + H₂O + light Glucose + O₂
Products Glucose + O₂ CO₂ + H₂O + ATP
Organelle Chloroplast Cytoplasm, then mitochondria
Who Autotrophs only Everyone
Electron carrier NADPH NADH

Details and Evidence

Glycolysis

  • "A metabolic pathway": the cell takes 1 glucose, splits it in half, and collects ATP and electrons.
  • Happens in all types of cells, prokaryotic and eukaryotic.
  • Happens in the cytoplasm.

$$\text{Glucose (6 C)} + 2\,ATP \rightarrow 4\,ATP + 2\,NADH + 2\,\text{pyruvate (3 C)}$$

Net gain: 2 ATP (4 made − 2 spent).

Phase What happens
1. Energy investment Glucose enters the cytoplasm. ATP attaches a phosphate → glucose-6-phosphate, which is rearranged to fructose-6-phosphate. A kinase uses a second ATP to add another phosphate → fructose-1,6-bisphosphate. This is the rate-determining step.
2. Energy generation The 6 C sugar splits into 2 glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (3 C each). These are converted to 2 pyruvate, making 4 ATP and 2 NADH.
  • Pyruvate needs O₂ to continue into the mitochondria (citric acid cycle), where most of the ATP is made.

[!warning] Fix from my notebook
My notes have "another ATP → fructose-6-phosphate". The second ATP is actually used one step later, to turn fructose-6-phosphate into fructose-1,6-bisphosphate. That phosphate-adding step belongs to the investment phase, not the generation phase.

Connections

  • Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P / PGAL) appears in both directions. The Calvin-Benson Cycle builds it from CO₂; glycolysis breaks glucose down into it.
  • Both processes use an electron transport chain and chemiosmosis to make ATP: compare Light Reactions.
  • ATP structure and why removing a phosphate releases energy: ATP and NADPH.
  • Respiration is where consumers lose most of their energy as heat: Energy Pyramid and the 10 Percent Rule.
  • Plants do this too: Autotrophs vs Heterotrophs.
  • Starch → glucose → ATP in a leaf kept in the dark: Activity 10 - Pigments and Energy Transformation in Leaf.
  • "Living things metabolize" in Characteristics of life.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- Where does glycolysis happen, and in which kinds of cells?
In the cytoplasm of all cells, prokaryotic and eukaryotic.

[!question]- What is the net ATP gain from glycolysis for one glucose?
2 ATP (2 invested, 4 produced), plus 2 NADH and 2 pyruvate.

[!question]- How are the equations for photosynthesis and cellular respiration related?
They are reverses of each other: the products of one are the reactants of the other.
