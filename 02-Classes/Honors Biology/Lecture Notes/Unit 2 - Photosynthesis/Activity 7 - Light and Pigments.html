Activity 7 - Light and Pigments

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
Part A: a chlorophyll extract looks green because it reflects green, but under a lamp in a dark room it glows red: absorbed energy coming back out as fluorescence. Part B: each plant pigment absorbs its own set of colors, and the color we see is whatever is not absorbed.

Part A: chlorophyll's interaction with light

Condition What I saw
1. Beaker in room light Dark green, not transparent, light green material suspended at the bottom
2. Beaker in a dark room Black, more uniform color, opaque
3. Lamp on in the dark room Green from the top, red from the bottom. A color gradient through the suspension

Explanation

  • Green from the top: green light is reflected. The rest of the light is absorbed.
  • Absorbed light = energy. It excites electrons in the chlorophyll.
  • Red: in a beaker there is nothing to accept the excited electrons, so they fall back and the energy is re-emitted as red light (fluorescence) plus heat.
  • In a living chloroplast that energy would have been captured instead. See Chlorophyll Excitation and Fluorescence.

Part B: absorption and reflection by different pigments

Sample Colors absorbed (shaded on my sheet) Colors that came through
Sweet potato red (partly), yellow, green, blue (partly), indigo, violet orange
Green pepper blue (partly), indigo, violet red, orange, yellow, green
Yellow pepper indigo, violet red to blue
Red pepper green (partly), blue, indigo, violet red, orange, yellow
Ethyl alcohol (the solvent) nothing: "everything" came through all colors
Sunlight (no sample) nothing: "all light present" all colors

[!info] How sure am I of this table
It is transcribed from light pencil shading, and my worksheet prints Yellow before Orange. Check it against the original page below before memorising it.

What the pattern means

  • Every colored extract absorbs the short-wavelength, high-energy end (indigo, violet). I wrote: "shorter λ = higher energy → higher-quality energy that could be used for biological processes."
  • The color of the vegetable is the color that is not absorbed: sweet potato lets orange through, red pepper lets red through.
  • Ethyl alcohol is the control. It is clear and absorbs nothing, so any absorption seen is from the pigment, not the solvent.
  • Sunlight shows the full spectrum for comparison.
  • A pure chlorophyll extract would also absorb red. My green pepper row shows only the blue-violet end, probably because the red band is harder to see by eye.

Connections

  • Color, wavelength and energy: Light and the Electromagnetic Spectrum.
  • Pigment types (chlorophylls, carotenoids, anthocyanin) and the proper absorption spectrum: Photosynthetic Pigments.
  • Reflected vs. absorbed vs. transmitted light: Energy Efficiency of Photosynthesis.
  • Sweet potato and pepper colors come from carotenoids, the same accessory pigments found in Photosystems.
  • Ethanol as the solvent: Amphipathic Molecules and Membranes.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- Why did the chlorophyll extract look red under the lamp in a dark room?
Fluorescence. The chlorophyll absorbed light, its electrons were excited, and with no electron acceptor present they fell back and released the energy as red light.

[!question]- Why was ethyl alcohol included in Part B?
As a control: it is the solvent, and it absorbs no visible light, so absorption in the other tubes must come from the pigments.

[!question]- An extract absorbs blue, indigo, violet and green. What color does it look?
A mix of what is left: red, orange and yellow, so it looks orange-red.
