Activity 8 - IKI Test for Starch

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
IKI (iodine–potassium iodide) only reacts with starch. It turns amylose dark blue-black because iodine gets trapped inside the coiled starch helix. Sugars with no helix (glucose, maltose) stay IKI's own yellow-orange.

Method

  • Four test tubes, each filled about two fingers deep: glucose, maltose, amylose, dH₂O.
  • 5 drops of IKI added to each.
  • dH₂O is the negative control (it shows what "no reaction" looks like).

Results

Tube Formula Observation Result
Glucose C₆H₁₂O₆ Light orange, clear Negative
Maltose C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ Lighter orange, clear Negative
Amylose Cₓ(H₂O)ᵧ Turned dark purple / blue / black, opaque Positive
dH₂O (control) H₂O Lightest orange, clear Negative

Why

  • The orange in the negative tubes is just the color of IKI itself.
  • Amylose is a long chain of glucose. Hydrogen bonds between its –OH groups make the chain coil into a helix.
  • Iodine is trapped inside the helix, and the trapped iodine appears dark blue.
  • Glucose (one ring) and maltose (two rings) are far too short to coil, so there is nothing to trap the iodine.
  • Amylose + iodine → blue. Add amylase → the starch is hydrolyzed to maltose and the blue disappears.

Full structural explanation: Carbohydrates and Glycosidic Bonds.

Why this test matters for photosynthesis

Plants store the glucose they make as starch. So a blue-black iodine stain on a leaf is evidence that the leaf has been photosynthesizing. That is exactly how the test is used in Activity 10 - Pigments and Energy Transformation in Leaf.

Connections

  • The other carbohydrate test, which gives the opposite pattern: Activity 9 - Benedict Test for Reducing Sugars.
  • Where the stored glucose came from: Calvin-Benson Cycle.
  • Hydrolysis of starch back to sugar when the plant needs energy: Glycolysis and Cellular Respiration.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- What does a positive IKI test look like, and what does it show?
Dark blue-black. It shows starch (amylose) is present.

[!question]- Why was distilled water tested?
As a negative control, to show the color of IKI when no reaction happens.

[!question]- Maltose is made of glucose, just like starch. Why is its IKI test negative?
Two glucose units are too short to form a helix, so iodine is not trapped.

[!question]- Starch + IKI is blue-black. You add amylase and wait. What happens and why?
The blue fades. Amylase hydrolyzes the starch into maltose, destroying the helix that held the iodine.
