Autotrophs vs Heterotrophs

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
Autotrophs make their own organic food from inorganic molecules (CO₂ and H₂O) using light. Heterotrophs must eat organic food made by something else. Both of them break glucose down with cellular respiration to get ATP.

Key Concepts

Autotroph (producer) Heterotroph (consumer)
Gets carbon from CO₂ (inorganic) Eating other organisms (organic)
Gets energy from Light Chemical energy in food
Does photosynthesis? Yes No
Does cellular respiration? Yes Yes
Examples Plants, algae, cyanobacteria, phytoplankton, Euglena Paramecium, shrimp, fish, birds, us
  • Photoautotroph = "light self-feeder": plants, cyanobacteria, most algae, phytoplankton (slide 6).
  • Not all autotrophs are plants: some bacteria (cyanobacteria) and protists (algae, Euglena) are too (slide 5).

Details and Evidence

What goes in and out (from my notebook diagram)

flowchart LR
    subgraph AUTO[Autotroph cell]
        PS[Photosynthesis] --> G1[Glucose]
        PS --> O2a[O₂]
        G1 --> CR1[Cellular respiration]
        O2a --> CR1
        CR1 --> ATP1[ATP]
    end
    L([Light]) --> PS
    W([H₂O]) --> PS
    C([CO₂]) --> PS
    O2a -->|extra O₂ leaves| OUT1([to the air])
    CR1 -->|CO₂| OUT1

    subgraph HET[Heterotroph cell]
        G2[Glucose from food] --> CR2[Cellular respiration]
        CR2 --> ATP2[ATP]
    end
    AUTO ==>|is eaten by| HET
    O2in([O₂ from the air]) --> CR2
    CR2 -->|CO₂ + H₂O + heat| OUT2([released])
    HET -->|cellulose, fiber: undigested| OUT3([feces])
  • The autotroph's two processes feed each other: photosynthesis makes glucose + O₂, respiration uses glucose + O₂ and gives back CO₂ + H₂O.
  • Light hitting a heterotroph does nothing useful for making food; it has no way to capture it.
  • The heterotroph loses energy as heat and loses cellulose (fiber) it cannot digest. That is the start of the 10% rule.

[!warning] Common trap
Plants do not only photosynthesize. They also do cellular respiration all the time, day and night, to make ATP from the glucose they built.

Connections

  • Autotrophs are the first step of every food chain.
  • How they do it: Photosynthesis Overview → Light Reactions + Calvin-Benson Cycle.
  • What both groups do with glucose: Glycolysis and Cellular Respiration.
  • Organisms I actually looked at: Activity 6 and 11 - Photosynthetic Autotrophs (Elodea, Cladophora, Oscillatoria, Spirogyra).
  • "Living things metabolize: exchange energy with the environment" in Characteristics of life.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- Is a mushroom an autotroph? Is a cyanobacterium?
A mushroom is a heterotroph (it absorbs food). A cyanobacterium is a photoautotroph even though it is a prokaryote with no chloroplasts.

[!question]- True or false: only heterotrophs carry out cellular respiration.
False. Autotrophs respire too; they just make their own glucose first.

[!question]- What does "inorganic carbon to organic carbon" mean?
CO₂ (no C–H bonds, inorganic) is built into glucose (an organic molecule). Only autotrophs can do this step: see carbon fixation.
