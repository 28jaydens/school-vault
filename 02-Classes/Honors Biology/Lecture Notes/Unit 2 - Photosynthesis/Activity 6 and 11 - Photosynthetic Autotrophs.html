Activity 6 and 11 - Photosynthetic Autotrophs

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
Four very different organisms, all photosynthetic autotrophs: a plant, two green algae and a cyanobacterium. Photosynthesis is not just a plant thing, and one of the four does it without chloroplasts.

The four organisms at a glance

Elodea Cladophora Spirogyra Oscillatoria
What it is Aquatic plant (leaf) Green alga, filament Green alga, filament Cyanobacterium
Cell type Eukaryote Eukaryote Eukaryote Prokaryote
Nucleus Yes Yes Yes (I drew it) No
Chloroplasts Many small ones, around the edge Many, denser at one end Spiral, ribbon-shaped, with pyrenoids None: pigments are on membranes in the cytoplasm
Size I measured cell ≈ 125 µm × 25 µm cell ≈ 300 µm × 57.5 µm field ≈ 300 µm at 400× filament ≈ 57.5 µm wide

Details and Evidence

Elodea (Fig. 1)

  • Labelled: chloroplast, cytoplasm, cell wall, cell membrane, extracellular matrix, vacuole.
  • Chloroplasts were moving: cytoplasmic streaming. The cytoplasm circulates and carries the chloroplasts around the cell.
  • The chloroplasts sit on the outside edge of the cell. They are pushed out by the large central vacuole, and being at the edge maximizes surface area for catching light and exchanging gases.

Cladophora (Fig. 2)

  • Labelled: cell wall, chloroplast, cytoplasm, ECM, cell membrane.
  • Cells look like green rectangles of roughly uniform size.
  • Chloroplasts were more concentrated at the bottom of the cell, near the connection to the next cell. (My open question: why not the top?)
  • A clear structure at the connection between cells.
  • Counted about 5 heterotrophs in view.

Oscillatoria (Fig. 3)

  • Labelled: extracellular matrix, cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm.
  • Bacteria → prokaryotic → no nucleus.
  • Green in different shades = different density of pigment.
  • Not many heterotrophs around.

[!warning] Fix from my notebook
I labelled a "chloroplast" in Oscillatoria. A cyanobacterium is a prokaryote, so it has no membrane-bound organelles: no nucleus and no chloroplasts. Its chlorophyll is on thylakoid membranes that lie free in the cytoplasm. On a test, the right label is something like "photosynthetic pigment / thylakoid membranes".

Spirogyra at 400× (Activity 11)

  • Labelled: chloroplast, nucleus, and what I wrote as "Tyranoid": the word is pyrenoid.
  • Pyrenoid: a dense protein body inside the chloroplast of many algae, where CO₂ is fixed and starch builds up around it.
  • The chloroplast is one long spiral ribbon running through the cell, which is where the name Spirogyra comes from.

Final statement

Relatively few heterotrophs compared to autotrophs.

That is the energy pyramid seen through a microscope: a large base of producers supports a much smaller number of consumers.

Measuring with the microscope

Size = number of eyepiece divisions × the value of one division at that magnification. My working: 120 × 2.5 = 300 µm and 23 × 2.5 = 57.5 µm.

Connections

  • Definitions: Autotrophs vs Heterotrophs.
  • What a chloroplast is and why Oscillatoria lacks one: Chloroplast Structure.
  • All four use chlorophyll: Photosynthetic Pigments.
  • Pyrenoids are where the Calvin-Benson Cycle enzyme RuBisCO is concentrated in algae.
  • Producers at the bottom of the food chain.
  • Levels of organisation and prokaryote vs. eukaryote: Characteristics of life.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- Which of the four organisms is a prokaryote, and how can you tell?
Oscillatoria. It has no nucleus and no chloroplasts.

[!question]- Why are Elodea's chloroplasts found around the edge of the cell?
The large central vacuole pushes them outward, and at the edge they get the most light and gas exchange.

[!question]- What is cytoplasmic streaming?
The flowing movement of cytoplasm inside a cell, which carries chloroplasts around.

[!question]- What is distinctive about the chloroplast of Spirogyra?
It is a spiral, ribbon-shaped chloroplast with pyrenoids.

[!question]- A cell measures 40 eyepiece divisions and each division is 2.5 µm. How long is it?
100 µm.
