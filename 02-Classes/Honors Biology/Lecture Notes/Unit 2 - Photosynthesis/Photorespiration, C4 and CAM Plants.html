Photorespiration, C4 and CAM Plants

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
Plants face a balancing act: open stomata let CO₂ in but let water out. On hot, dry days stomata close, CO₂ runs low inside the leaf, and RuBisCO starts grabbing O₂ instead of CO₂. That wasteful process is photorespiration. C4 and CAM plants have adaptations that avoid it.

[!info] Scope
This is in the slides (60–63) but not on the Test 2 outline, and my notebook has nothing on it. Know the main idea and the one-line difference between C4 and CAM.

Key Concepts

  • Photorespiration: RuBisCO attaches O₂ to RuBP instead of CO₂. The plant uses ATP, releases CO₂, and makes no sugar. It undoes some of the work of photosynthesis.
  • When: hot, dry, bright conditions. Stomata close to save water → CO₂ inside the leaf drops and O₂ from the light reactions builds up.
  • C3 plants: "normal" plants. CO₂ is fixed directly by RuBisCO into a 3-carbon compound (PGA). Most affected by photorespiration.

Details and Evidence

Calvin cycle vs. photorespiration (slide 62)

RuBisCO + CO₂ RuBisCO + O₂
Called Calvin cycle (carbon fixation) Photorespiration
Makes 2 PGA → sugar 1 PGA + a 2-carbon compound that has to be recycled
Net effect Gains carbon Loses CO₂, uses ATP

The two adaptations (slide 63)

C4 plants CAM plants
Strategy Spatial separation: two steps in two different places Temporal separation: two steps at two different times
Step 1 In mesophyll cells, the enzyme PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ into a 4-carbon compound. PEP carboxylase does not react with O₂. At night, stomata open. CO₂ is fixed into a 4-carbon acid (malic acid) and stored in the vacuole.
Step 2 The 4 C compound moves into bundle-sheath cells and releases its CO₂ right next to RuBisCO → Calvin cycle. During the day, stomata stay closed. The acid releases CO₂ → Calvin cycle, powered by the light reactions.
Result RuBisCO always sits in high CO₂ Very little water lost
Examples Corn, sugarcane Cactus, pineapple, other desert plants 
flowchart TD
    HOT[Hot, dry day] --> CLOSE[Stomata close to save water]
    CLOSE --> LOW[CO₂ falls and O₂ rises inside the leaf]
    LOW --> PR[RuBisCO binds O₂: photorespiration]
    PR --> WASTE[ATP used, CO₂ lost, no sugar]
    LOW -. avoided by .-> C4P[C4 plants: fix CO₂ in mesophyll,<br/>Calvin cycle in bundle sheath]
    LOW -. avoided by .-> CAMP[CAM plants: fix CO₂ at night,<br/>Calvin cycle by day]

The balancing act (slides 60–61)

  • Transpiration: water evaporating from the leaf through the stomata. It cools the plant and pulls water up from the roots, but it also dries the plant out.
  • In a drought, plants must keep stomata shut more often, so they fix less carbon. Climate affects how much photosynthesis can happen.

Connections

  • The enzyme at the centre of all this: RuBisCO in the Calvin-Benson Cycle.
  • The gate that causes the problem: Stomata and Transpiration, and my lab: Lab - Water Retention in Plants.
  • Bundle-sheath and mesophyll cells are in the leaf cross-section.
  • Photorespiration is one of the losses in Energy Efficiency of Photosynthesis.
  • It is a homeostasis trade-off (water balance vs. food making): Characteristics of life.

Check yourself

[!question]- What is photorespiration and why is it a problem?
RuBisCO binds O₂ instead of CO₂. It uses ATP and releases CO₂ without making sugar, so it wastes energy and carbon.

[!question]- In one line each, how do C4 and CAM plants avoid it?
C4: separate the steps in space (fix CO₂ in mesophyll, run the Calvin cycle in bundle-sheath cells). CAM: separate them in time (fix CO₂ at night, run the Calvin cycle in the day with stomata closed).

[!question]- Why does photorespiration get worse on hot, dry days?
Stomata close to save water, so CO₂ cannot enter and O₂ cannot leave; the low CO₂ : O₂ ratio makes RuBisCO bind O₂ more often.
