Amphipathic Molecules and Membranes

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
An amphipathic molecule has both a hydrophilic (water-loving, polar) region and a hydrophobic (water-fearing, non-polar) region. Phospholipids are amphipathic, which is why they form membranes. Ethanol is amphipathic too, which is why it can disrupt the thylakoid membrane and release the pigments held in it.

Key Concepts

Molecule Hydrophilic part (polar) Hydrophobic part (non-polar)
Ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH hydroxyl group, –OH the carbon–hydrogen end (CH₃CH₂–; "methyl" in my notes)
Phospholipid phosphate head (PO₄⁻) long fatty-acid tails (carbon–hydrogen chains)
  • Hydrophilic = polar, mixes with water.
  • Hydrophobic = non-polar, avoids water, mixes with fats and oils.
  • Amphipathic = has both kinds of region in one molecule.

Details and Evidence

Membranes

  • In water, phospholipids line up in a bilayer: heads facing the water on both sides, tails hidden in the middle.
  • The thylakoid membrane is this kind of bilayer. Chlorophyll's hydrophobic phytol tail and the carotenoids are anchored in its hydrophobic middle, together with the Photosystems.

Why ethanol pulls pigment out of a leaf

flowchart LR
    E[Hot ethanol:<br/>amphipathic] --> D[Slips between the phospholipids<br/>and disrupts the membrane]
    D --> R[Membrane-bound pigments<br/>are released]
    R --> G[Solution turns green]
    R --> W[Leaf turns white:<br/>no pigment left]
  • Water alone cannot do this: chlorophyll is hydrophobic and the membrane stays intact.
  • Water does remove anthocyanin (the pink pigment), because anthocyanin is water-soluble and lives in the vacuole, not in a membrane. That fits what I recorded: the leaf went into water first, and "pink color is gone".
  • Once the leaf is white, the iodine color change is easy to see. "The ethanol bath is needed to make the reaction more evident."

Lab safety point

Ethanol is flammable. It is heated in a hot water bath, never over a direct flame, so that it does not catch fire.

Connections

  • The lab that used this: Activity 10 - Pigments and Energy Transformation in Leaf.
  • Which pigments are lipid-soluble and which are water-soluble: Photosynthetic Pigments.
  • Where the membrane is: Chloroplast Structure.
  • The membrane has to be intact for the H⁺ gradient in the Light Reactions. A disrupted membrane means no chemiosmosis.
  • A "phospholipid membrane" is one of the features of the first living organism in my Orgins of LIfe diagram.
  • Polarity and hydrogen bonding also explain starch: Carbohydrates and Glycosidic Bonds.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- What does amphipathic mean? Give two examples from this unit.
Having both a hydrophilic and a hydrophobic region. Ethanol and phospholipids.

[!question]- Why does boiling a leaf in ethanol turn the ethanol green and the leaf white?
Ethanol disrupts the thylakoid membranes, releasing the membrane-bound chlorophyll, which dissolves in the ethanol. The leaf is left without pigment.

[!question]- Why does the pink color leave in water but the green does not?
Anthocyanin is water-soluble (in the vacuole). Chlorophyll is hydrophobic and locked in a membrane, so it needs ethanol.
