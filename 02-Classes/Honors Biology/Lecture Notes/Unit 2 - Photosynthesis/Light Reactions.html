Light Reactions

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
The light reactions convert solar energy into chemical energy. In the thylakoid membrane, light drives electrons from water through two photosystems and an electron transport chain. The results are ATP, NADPH and O₂. ATP and NADPH then power the Calvin-Benson Cycle.

Key Concepts

  • Where: thylakoid membrane (stacks of thylakoids = grana). The stroma is outside the thylakoid.
  • Inputs: light, H₂O, NADP⁺, ADP + Pᵢ.
  • Outputs: O₂ (leaves the plant), ATP and NADPH (go to the stroma).
  • Photolysis: $2\,H_2O \rightarrow 4\,H^+ + O_2 + 4\,e^-$. Those electrons are the important product.
  • Chemiosmosis: H⁺ flowing down its concentration gradient through ATP synthase makes ATP.
  • Photophosphorylation: adding a phosphate to ADP using light energy.

Details and Evidence

Noncyclic electron flow, step by step

  1. PSII (P680) absorbs light. An excited electron leaves for the primary electron acceptor.
  2. Water is split (photolysis) to replace it: 2 H₂O → 4 H⁺ + O₂ + 4 e⁻. The O₂ diffuses out; the H⁺ stays in the thylakoid lumen.
  3. Electron transport chain: PSII → plastoquinone (Pq) → cytochrome complex → plastocyanin (Pc) → PSI.
  4. H⁺ pumping: plastoquinone and the cytochrome complex use the electrons' energy to bring H⁺ from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen. (Slide note: plastoquinone = the hydrogen pump.)
  5. PSI (P700) absorbs light and re-excites the electrons.
  6. PSI → ferredoxin (Fd) → ferredoxin–NADP⁺ reductase: $NADP^+ + H^+ + 2\,e^- \rightarrow NADPH$. NADPH is made in the stroma, ready for the dark reactions.
  7. Chemiosmosis: H⁺ is now concentrated in the lumen. It flows back to the stroma through ATP synthase, which makes ADP + Pᵢ → ATP.

Chemiosmosis

Thylakoid lumen Stroma
H⁺ concentration High Low
How H⁺ got there Water splitting + pumping by Pq / cytochrome
What happens H⁺ flows out through ATP synthase → ATP is made here

Three things make the gradient steeper: water is split inside the lumen (adds H⁺), the ETC pumps H⁺ in, and NADP⁺ uses up H⁺ in the stroma.

Noncyclic vs. cyclic photophosphorylation

Noncyclic Cyclic
Photosystems used PSII and PSI PSI only (P700)
Electron path H₂O → PSII → ETC → PSI → NADP⁺ (one way) PSI → ferredoxin → cytochrome complex → plastocyanin → back to PSI
Makes ATP? Yes Yes
Makes NADPH? Yes No
Splits water / releases O₂? Yes No

Why cyclic exists (slide 56): ATP is a limiting factor. The Calvin cycle uses more ATP than NADPH (18 ATP vs. 12 NADPH per glucose), so the plant needs a way to fill the ATP deficit without making extra NADPH. Slide 57 also notes cyclic photophosphorylation is how some photosynthetic bacteria make ATP.

[!warning] Common traps
- Order is PSII → PSI, not I then II.
- O₂ comes from water, split at PSII.
- H⁺ is high inside the thylakoid, and ATP is made on the stroma side.
- NADPH carries electrons (reducing power); ATP carries energy. The Calvin cycle needs both.

Connections

  • Energy-level view of the same pathway: Z Scheme.
  • The machinery: Photosystems, in the membranes of the chloroplast.
  • The carriers produced: ATP and NADPH.
  • Where the products are used: Calvin-Benson Cycle.
  • First step in detail: Chlorophyll Excitation and Fluorescence.
  • Cellular respiration also uses an electron transport chain and chemiosmosis: Glycolysis and Cellular Respiration.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- List the electron carriers in order from PSII to NADPH.
PSII → plastoquinone → cytochrome complex → plastocyanin → PSI → ferredoxin → NADP⁺ reductase → NADPH.

[!question]- What are the three products of the light reactions, and where does each go?
O₂ (diffuses out of the leaf), ATP and NADPH (used by the Calvin cycle in the stroma).

[!question]- Explain chemiosmosis in the chloroplast in two sentences.
The electron transport chain pumps H⁺ into the thylakoid lumen, building a high concentration there. H⁺ then flows back to the stroma through ATP synthase, and that flow powers ADP + Pᵢ → ATP.

[!question]- How does cyclic electron flow differ from noncyclic?
It uses only PSI; electrons loop from ferredoxin back to the cytochrome complex and PSI. It makes ATP but no NADPH and no O₂.

[!question]- A chemical makes the thylakoid membrane leaky to H⁺. What happens to ATP production?
It drops or stops, because the H⁺ gradient cannot build up, so nothing drives ATP synthase.
