Calvin-Benson Cycle

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
The Calvin–Benson cycle (also called the dark reactions or light-independent reactions) makes sugar from CO₂ in the stroma. This is carbon fixation: inorganic carbon (CO₂) is fixed into an organic molecule. ATP from the light reactions supplies the energy and NADPH supplies the electrons.

Key Concepts

  • Where: stroma of the chloroplast.
  • Cycle: it starts and ends with the same molecule, RuBP.
  • RuBisCO: the enzyme that joins CO₂ to RuBP.
  • Carbon fixation: converting inorganic CO₂ into an organic compound.
  • Energy transformation: chemical energy in ATP and NADPH → chemical (potential) energy stored in the bonds of glucose.

Details and Evidence

Three phases

Phase What happens Uses
1. Carbon fixation CO₂ (1 C) + RuBP (5 C) → unstable 6 C intermediate → splits into 2 PGA (3 C each). Enzyme: RuBisCO. CO₂
2. Reduction PGA is reduced to PGAL (G3P), a 3 C sugar. NADPH → NADP⁺ + 2 e⁻ + H⁺; ATP → ADP + Pᵢ. ATP, NADPH
3. Regeneration of RuBP Most PGAL is rearranged back into RuBP so the cycle can start again. ATP

The "used" ADP, Pᵢ and NADP⁺ go back to the Light Reactions to be recharged.

Carbon bookkeeping for one glucose (the numbers in my notebook)

Step Molecules Carbons
Start 6 RuBP (5 C) 30 C
Add 6 CO₂ (1 C) + 6 C
After fixation 6 unstable 6 C → 12 PGA (3 C) 36 C
After reduction 12 PGAL (3 C) 36 C
Leaves the cycle 2 PGAL → 1 glucose (6 C) 6 C out
Regenerated 10 PGAL → 6 RuBP 30 C back to start

Totals per glucose: 6 CO₂ + 18 ATP + 12 NADPH → 1 glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆).
The slides draw the half-size version: 3 CO₂ + 9 ATP + 6 NADPH → 1 G3P.

Names that mean the same thing

In my notes Also called
PGA 3-PGA, 3-phosphoglycerate
PGAL G3P, glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate
RuBP ribulose bisphosphate
Dark reactions light-independent reactions, Calvin cycle

After the cycle

Glucose leaves the chloroplast with the help of a transport protein. It can be burned in cellular respiration, built into cellulose, or stored as starch (Carbohydrates and Glycosidic Bonds). Starch is broken back down to sugar by hydrolysis (amylase + H₂O).

[!warning] Fixes from my notebook
- I wrote "6 cycles for 1 CO₂". It is the other way round: each turn fixes 1 CO₂, so 6 turns make 1 glucose (which matches "1 glucose = 6 × CO₂" on the same page).
- I labelled PGAL "(oxidized)". PGAL is the reduced product. PGA gains electrons from NADPH; it is NADPH that gets oxidized to NADP⁺.
- "Dark reaction" does not mean it happens at night. It cannot keep going in the dark, because it needs the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.

Connections

  • Supplies of ATP and NADPH: Light Reactions and ATP and NADPH.
  • CO₂ gets in through the stomata: Stomata and Transpiration. Closed stomata = no CO₂ = slower Calvin cycle.
  • When RuBisCO grabs O₂ instead of CO₂: Photorespiration, C4 and CAM Plants.
  • What happens to the glucose: Carbohydrates and Glycosidic Bonds, Glycolysis and Cellular Respiration.
  • Proof a leaf has been fixing carbon: starch turns black with iodine in Activity 10 - Pigments and Energy Transformation in Leaf.
  • CO₂ is reduced to sugar: redox.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- Name the three phases of the Calvin cycle in order.
Carbon fixation, reduction, regeneration of RuBP.

[!question]- What does RuBisCO do?
It attaches CO₂ to RuBP (5 C), making an unstable 6 C molecule that splits into two PGA.

[!question]- How many turns, ATP and NADPH does it take to make one glucose?
6 turns (6 CO₂), 18 ATP and 12 NADPH.

[!question]- What do ATP and NADPH each contribute?
ATP provides energy; NADPH provides the electrons (and H) that reduce CO₂ to sugar.

[!question]- The lights go off. Why does the Calvin cycle stop a short time later?
No light means no new ATP or NADPH from the light reactions, and the cycle cannot run without them.
