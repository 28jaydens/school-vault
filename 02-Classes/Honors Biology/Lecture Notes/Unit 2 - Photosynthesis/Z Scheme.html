Z Scheme

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
The Z scheme is a graph of the electrons' energy level as they move through the light reactions. Light lifts the electrons twice (at PSII and at PSI). Between the lifts they fall "downhill" through carriers, and that released energy is used to make ATP. The path looks like a Z on its side.

Key Concepts

  • y-axis: energy level of the electron. x-axis: sequence of carriers.
  • Going up needs energy: a photon absorbed at P680, then at P700.
  • Going down releases energy: used to pump H⁺ (→ ATP) on the first slope, and to reduce NADP⁺ on the second.
  • Electrons start in water (lowest energy) and finish in NADPH (high energy). Light supplied the difference.

Details and Evidence

Letter in my diagram Carrier What happens
PSII P680 Light excites 2 e⁻; H₂O → 2 e⁻ + 2 H⁺ + ½ O₂ replaces them
A Primary electron acceptor of PSII Catches the excited electrons (top of the first lift)
B Plastoquinone (Pq) Downhill; carries H⁺ across the membrane
C Cytochrome complex Downhill; pumps H⁺ into the lumen → ATP by chemiosmosis
D Plastocyanin (Pc) Delivers electrons to PSI
PSI P700 Light excites the electrons a second time
E Primary electron acceptor of PSI Top of the second lift (highest energy on the graph)
F Ferredoxin (Fd) Passes electrons to the enzyme
NADP⁺ reductase NADP⁺ + H⁺ + 2 e⁻ → NADPH

[!info] About the letters
The circled letters A–F are from the graph in my notebook. The carrier names are matched from my light-reaction notes and slide 27, in the same order. If the class handout labels them differently, go with the handout.

Reading the graph

  • Two steep rises = two photons absorbed (one per photosystem, per electron).
  • First downhill (A → D) = electron transport chain. Energy released here builds the H⁺ gradient → ATP.
  • Second downhill (E → NADP⁺) = electrons end up stored in NADPH.
  • Dashed line = cyclic flow: electrons from ferredoxin drop back to the cytochrome complex and return to PSI, making extra ATP without NADPH or O₂.

[!warning] Fix from my notebook
Next to PSII my graph looks like it says water gives "½ O₂ + 2 H₂". The product is 2 H⁺ (protons), not hydrogen gas: H₂O → 2 e⁻ + 2 H⁺ + ½ O₂.

Connections

  • Same pathway drawn in the membrane: Light Reactions.
  • Why an electron goes up at all: Chlorophyll Excitation and Fluorescence.
  • P680 and P700: Photosystems.
  • "Falling electrons release energy" is oxidation–reduction: redox.
  • Where NADPH and ATP go next: Calvin-Benson Cycle.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- What do the two upward arrows on the Z scheme represent?
Absorption of light energy at PSII (P680) and at PSI (P700), each raising the electrons to a higher energy level.

[!question]- Where on the Z scheme is ATP made, and where is NADPH made?
ATP: during the downhill run between PSII and PSI (electron transport chain). NADPH: at the end, after PSI.

[!question]- Where do the electrons come from, and where do they end up?
From water (split at PSII). They end up in NADPH.

[!question]- Why are two photosystems needed instead of one?
One photon does not give an electron enough energy both to pump H⁺ for ATP and to reduce NADP⁺. Two boosts in series do both.
