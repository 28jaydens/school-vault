Stomata and Transpiration

← Unit 2 hub · Test 2 master notes · Class home

[!summary] Big idea
A stoma (plural stomata) is a pore in the leaf surface, controlled by two guard cells. Stomata let CO₂ in for photosynthesis, but water vapor escapes through the same opening. Opening and closing them is how a plant balances making food against drying out.

Key Concepts

  • Stoma: the hole. Guard cells: the pair of cells that open and close it.
  • Guard cells are green: they contain chloroplasts.
  • Epidermal cells around them are transparent: no chloroplasts, so light passes through to the cells below.
  • Transpiration: loss of water vapor from the leaf, mostly through open stomata.

Details and Evidence

What moves through a stoma

Substance Direction Why
CO₂ In (diffusion) Needed for the Calvin-Benson Cycle
O₂ Out Made by the Light Reactions
H₂O vapor Out Transpiration

What controls opening and closing

  • Light: stomata usually open in light (CO₂ is needed) and close in the dark.
  • Water: with plenty of water the guard cells swell (turgid), bow apart and the pore opens. When the plant is short of water they go limp (flaccid) and the pore closes.

Where the stomata are

  • Land plants: more stomata on the underside of the leaf. It is shaded and cooler, so water leaves more slowly.
  • Floating water plants: the opposite. Stomata are on the top, because the underside is in the water.

What else affects water loss

  • Cuticle and wax. Leaves have different thicknesses of waxy cuticle. A thicker cuticle prevents more evaporation.
  • Leaf size. The larger the leaf (more surface area), the faster it dries.
  • Hotter, drier, windier and brighter conditions all increase transpiration.

The trade-off

Stomata open Stomata closed
CO₂ gets in → photosynthesis runs No CO₂ → photosynthesis slows
Water is lost Water is saved
O₂ builds up → risk of photorespiration

Connections

  • This is a stimulus → response system with negative feedback: water loss is the stimulus, closing the stomata reduces further water loss. See Stimulus & positive negative feedback.
  • Keeping water balance is homeostasis: Characteristics of life.
  • Transpiration is also the pull that moves water up the plant: Leaf Structure and Water Transport.
  • My measurements and observations: Lab - Water Retention in Plants.
  • How desert plants deal with it: Photorespiration, C4 and CAM Plants.

From my notebook

Check yourself

[!question]- Why are most stomata on the underside of a land plant's leaf?
The underside is shaded and cooler, so less water evaporates there.

[!question]- What is different about guard cells compared with other epidermal cells?
Guard cells contain chloroplasts and can change shape to open or close the pore. Ordinary epidermal cells are transparent with no chloroplasts.

[!question]- A plant closes its stomata on a hot afternoon. Give one benefit and one cost.
Benefit: less water lost by transpiration. Cost: CO₂ cannot enter, so the Calvin cycle slows (and photorespiration increases).

[!question]- Two leaves are left to dry: one large and thin, one small with a thick waxy cuticle. Which loses mass faster and why?
The large thin leaf. More surface area and less wax means faster evaporation.
