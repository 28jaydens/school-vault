Lab 2A - Pigment Chromatography

Updated Nov 17, 2026·615 words · 3 min read
#class/bio#type/lecture#bio/unit-3#lab

← Unit 3 hub · Test 3 review · Class home

Big idea

Four leaf pigments separate on paper because they have different functional groups. More hydrophobic groups → more soluble in the nonpolar solvent → higher Rf. Carotene (all hydrocarbon) ran to the top; the chlorophylls (polar carbonyls, Mg-porphyrin ring) stayed low. Chlorophyll a and b differ by one group, a methyl vs an aldehyde, and that alone changes their Rf.

This is Part A of Lab 2 (Separation of Biomolecules). Part B is Lab 2B - Gel Electrophoresis of Dyes.

My Rf values (solvent front = 6.3 cm)#

Pigment Colour on paper Distance Rf = d_pigment / d_solvent
Chlorophyll a blue-green 2.3 cm 0.37
Chlorophyll b yellow-green 3.1 cm 0.49
Xanthophyll yellow 3.5 cm 0.56
Carotene orange 6.2 cm 0.98
Check my a/b labels before the test

The usual order from a nonpolar solvent is carotene > xanthophyll > chlorophyll a > chlorophyll b, because chlorophyll b has the polar aldehyde and is held back most. My sheet gives chlorophyll a the lower Rf (0.37) and b the higher (0.49), which is the reverse. Either I swapped the two green bands when labelling, or this solvent behaves differently. Ask the teacher which order they expect; the reasoning in question (c) below is what they will grade.

Worksheet questions (Plant Pigments Worksheet #2)#

(a) Functional groups on each pigment#

Pigment Hydrophobic groups (red) Hydrophilic groups (blue)
Chlorophyll a Many methyl (–CH₃) groups, the long phytol hydrocarbon tail, the vinyl (CH₂=CH–) group Carbonyl C=O groups (ketone on the ring, ester C=O), the Mg-porphyrin ring with its N atoms
Chlorophyll b Same as a Same as a plus an aldehyde (–CHO) where a has a methyl
Xanthophyll Methyl groups and the long conjugated hydrocarbon chain A hydroxyl (–OH) on each end ring
Carotene Methyl groups and the long hydrocarbon chain, nothing else None

(b) Correlation between functional groups, Rf and position#

  • The more hydrophobic a pigment is, the more soluble it is in the nonpolar solvent and the less it sticks to the polar cellulose paper, so it travels farther and has a higher Rf. Carotene has only hydrocarbon groups → Rf 0.98, right at the solvent front.
  • Adding hydrophilic groups lowers the Rf. Xanthophyll is carotene plus two –OH groups → Rf 0.56. The chlorophylls carry polar carbonyls and the charged Mg ring → lowest Rfs, nearest the baseline.

(c) Chlorophyll a vs chlorophyll b#

  • They differ at one ring position: chlorophyll a has a methyl (–CH₃), chlorophyll b has an aldehyde (–CHO).
  • The aldehyde's C=O is polar, so chlorophyll b is more polar (more hydrophilic), dissolves less in the nonpolar solvent and binds the paper more. It should therefore travel less far and have the lower Rf of the two.

Connections#

  • The theory and how to measure Rf: Paper Chromatography and Rf.
  • The polarity rules: Functional Groups.
  • Why these pigments exist and what they absorb: Photosynthetic Pigments, Activity 7 - Light and Pigments.
  • Part B of this lab: Lab 2B - Gel Electrophoresis of Dyes.

From my notebook#

Check yourself#

Which pigment would travel farthest in a polar solvent, and why?

Chlorophyll b (or whichever chlorophyll is most polar). In a polar solvent the polar molecules dissolve best; carotene would barely move.

Two students get Rf 0.98 and 0.95 for carotene on different days. Is that a problem?

Not much: Rf depends on exact paper, solvent and temperature. The order of the pigments is what should be stable.

Name the single structural difference between chlorophyll a and b.

Methyl group in a, aldehyde group in b, at the same ring position.
