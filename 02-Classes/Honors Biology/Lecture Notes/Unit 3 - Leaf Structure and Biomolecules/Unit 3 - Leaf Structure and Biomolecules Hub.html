The whole unit in eight sentences
  1. A leaf is an organ built from tissues: cuticle, upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll (most photosynthesis), spongy mesophyll (gas exchange), vascular bundle (xylem water up, phloem sugar both ways), lower epidermis, guard cells around stomata.
  2. Almost all stomata are on the lower surface. We proved it with counts and 95% confidence intervals (mean ± 2 SE): non-overlapping error bars mean a significant difference.
  3. Accuracy is closeness to the true value; precision is closeness to each other. SD = spread of data, SE = SD/√n = spread of the mean.
  4. Photosynthesis is set by its limiting factor: temperature, CO₂, water, light (intensity and wavelength), chlorophyll. Hot, dry, bright days cause photorespiration because RuBisCO grabs O₂ instead of CO₂.
  5. C4 plants separate CO₂ capture (mesophyll, PEP) from the Calvin cycle (bundle sheath, Kranz anatomy) in space; CAM plants separate them in time (stomata open at night).
  6. Enzymes are organic catalysts that lower activation energy by holding the substrate in an active site; they are specific and reusable. RuBisCO is unusually unspecific.
  7. Functional groups decide behaviour: hydroxyl, carbonyl, carboxyl, amino, phosphate, sulfhydryl are polar/charged (hydrophilic); methyl is nonpolar (hydrophobic).
  8. Two ways to separate biomolecules: paper chromatography (by solubility; Rf = d_pigment/d_solvent) and gel electrophoresis (by charge, size, shape; negative runs to +). The Biuret test turns lilac/purple with protein.

Mixed self-test#

  1. Order the leaf layers from top to bottom.

Cuticle, upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll (with vascular bundles), lower epidermis with guard cells and stomata.

  1. Which transports water, which transports sugar, and in which directions?

Xylem: water, up only. Phloem: sugar (sap), both directions.

  1. Error bars of two bars overlap. What can you conclude?

That the difference is not statistically significant at the 95% level.

  1. What is SE and why does it shrink with more samples?

The spread of the mean if the experiment were repeated; SE = SD/√n, so bigger n → smaller SE.

  1. The rate-vs-light curve has flattened. Name two things you could change to find the limiting factor.

Raise CO₂ concentration or raise temperature (one at a time, everything else constant).

  1. Why is photorespiration worse on a hot, dry, sunny day?

Stomata close (CO₂ falls) while fast light reactions raise O₂, so RuBisCO binds O₂.

  1. C4 vs CAM in one phrase each.

C4: separation in space (mesophyll vs bundle sheath). CAM: separation in time (night vs day).

  1. Define active site.

The pocket of an enzyme where the substrate binds and the reaction happens.

  1. Which functional groups are charged, and which way does each shift pH?

Carboxyl and phosphate release H⁺ (acidic); amino accepts H⁺ (basic).

  1. Why does carotene have the highest Rf?

It is entirely hydrocarbon (nonpolar), so it is most soluble in the nonpolar solvent and least attracted to the polar paper.

  1. What single group differs between chlorophyll a and b, and what does it do to solubility?

Methyl (a) vs aldehyde (b). The aldehyde makes b more polar, so it dissolves less in a nonpolar solvent and travels less far.

  1. Why does DNA move toward the positive electrode?

Its phosphate groups give it a negative charge.

  1. A dye moved toward the negative electrode. Charge?

Positive.

  1. Name the three factors that decide how far a molecule moves in a gel.

Charge, size (smaller is faster), shape/complexity (simpler is faster).

  1. What did Calvin and Benson do at each time point, and why alcohol?

Drained a sample into boiling alcohol to kill the algae instantly (denatures enzymes), then separated its molecules by paper chromatography.

  1. Biuret test: reagent colour, positive colour, what the test detects, one safety point.

Light blue; lilac to purple; proteins (peptide bonds); the reagent is a strong base, wash it off.

  1. Why centrifuge the whey shake before testing?

To separate the cloudy solids into a pellet so the Biuret colour can be read in the clear supernatant.

  1. What is the null hypothesis in the Whey Elite investigation, and what did the evidence say?

That expiration made no difference and protein is still present. The whey turned lilac, so protein is still there; the "protein is gone" hypothesis was not supported.

  • Redraw the leaf cross-section and label all seven layers from memory.
  • Recalculate SD, SE and a 95% CI for my five eudicot counts without looking.
  • Sketch the limiting-factor graph with the three curves labelled.
  • Draw C4 (two cells) and CAM (night/day) from memory.
  • Write the functional-groups table from memory, then check polarity and charge.
  • Say the chromatography and electrophoresis "what separates them" rule aloud: solubility vs charge/size/shape.
