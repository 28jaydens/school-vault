Activity 13 - Biuret Test for Proteins

Updated Nov 17, 2026·784 words · 4 min read
#class/bio#type/lecture#bio/unit-3#activity

← Unit 3 hub · Test 3 review · Class home

Big idea

The Biuret test is a qualitative (and roughly quantitative) test for proteins. The reagent is light blue; with protein it turns lilac → purple-blue, and the darker the purple, the more protein. Water stays light blue (negative control). It joins IKI (starch) and Benedict's (reducing sugars) as the third biomolecule test.

Procedure#

Safety

Biuret reagent is a strong base. If it touches your fingers, wash them off immediately. Wash hands at the end.

  1. Put 2 mL of each test substance in its own test tube: gelatin, albumin (egg white), dH₂O. Record the starting colour.
  2. Gently roll 10 drops of Biuret reagent down the side of the test tube.
  3. Stand the tube upright. Do NOT mix. Record the final colour and where the colour appears.
  4. Dispose into the sink and wash your hands.

Table 1: observations of colour change#

Substance Starting colour Final colour Protein?
Gelatin Clear, transparent, no colour Lilac, light purple-blue Yes (weaker positive)
Albumin (egg white) Cloudy white, translucent Purple-blue, the darkest solution Yes (strongest positive)
dH₂O Clear, transparent, no colour Light blue No (negative control: the blue is just the reagent)

Reading the colours: blue = no protein; lilac/purple = protein; darker purple = more protein. That is why the test can be semi-quantitative.

Part B: the expired whey protein#

Phenomenon. Dr. Hsu notices that the "Protein Shake" mixture he is using, Whey Elite, has an expiration date of 9/2021.

Questions. Is there still protein in his shakes? What exactly does the expiration date mean in the case of Whey Elite?

Hypothesis. The expiration date indicates that the protein in the product has chemically altered and no longer provides the protein advertised.

Null hypothesis. The "no effect" statement the experiment tries to reject: expiration has made no difference, and the whey still contains protein.

Prediction (if, then). If the protein has broken down, then the Biuret test on the whey will stay light blue like water. If protein is still present, it will turn lilac/purple like gelatin.

Procedure. As given (same as Table 1, with whey protein as the test substance).

Table 2: whey protein results#

Substance Initial colour Final colour
dH₂O Clear, transparent, no colour Light blue
Gelatin Clear, transparent, no colour Lilac, light purple-blue
Whey protein Cloudy, opaque beige/yellow, whiter at the top Only a lilac colour at the bottom; the rest of the solution still cloudy yellow

Conclusion (address the hypothesis, cite evidence)#

  • The whey sample did turn lilac where the reagent reached it, the same colour as the gelatin positive control, while water stayed light blue. So protein (peptide bonds) is still present: the hypothesis that the protein "no longer provides the protein advertised" is not supported by this test.
  • The expiration date more likely reflects flavour, texture, mixing quality or vitamin content, not the disappearance of protein.
  • Limits: the cloudy yellow mixture hid most of the colour change, so the test only showed a positive at the bottom, and the colour cannot be compared fairly against the clear gelatin.

Improvement: centrifuge first#

Murky mixture → centrifugation → supernatant (liquid on top) + pellet (solid at the bottom) → run the Biuret test on the clear supernatant, where the colour is easier to see. Other improvements: test a fresh, unexpired Whey Elite as a positive control, and compare colours quantitatively (a colour scale or a spectrophotometer).

Connections#

  • The other two biomolecule tests from Unit 2: Activity 8 - IKI Test for Starch, Activity 9 - Benedict Test for Reducing Sugars.
  • Proteins are built from amino acids with amino and carboxyl groups: Functional Groups.
  • Enzymes are proteins: Enzymes and RuBisCO.

From my notebook#

Check yourself#

What colour means negative, and what colour means positive, in the Biuret test?

Light blue = no protein. Lilac to purple = protein, darker with more protein.

Why include a dH₂O tube?

Negative control: it shows what the reagent looks like with no protein, so any purple in the other tubes is real.

Why roll the reagent down the side and not mix?

So the reagent layers on top and the colour change forms at the interface where it is easy to see and compare between tubes.

What would centrifuging the whey shake achieve?

Separate the cloudy solids (pellet) from the liquid (supernatant), so the Biuret colour can be seen clearly in the clear liquid.

All three tests side by side: which reagent, which molecule, which positive colour?

IKI → starch → blue-black. Benedict's → reducing sugars → green/orange/brick red when heated. Biuret → protein → lilac/purple.
