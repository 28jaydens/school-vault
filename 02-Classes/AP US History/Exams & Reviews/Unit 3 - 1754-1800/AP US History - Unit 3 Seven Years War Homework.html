AP US History — Unit 3: Seven Years' War Homework

Updated Oct 28, 2026·1,537 words · 8 min read

← Unit 3 Hub

Causes of the Seven Years' War#

  • Britain and France competed for control of land, trade, and influence in North America.
  • The Ohio Valley became a major source of conflict because both Britain and France claimed the region.
  • British colonists and land companies wanted to expand westward into the Ohio Valley.
  • In 1749, Virginia gave the Ohio Company a huge land grant of about 500,000 acres in the region.
  • France responded by strengthening its forts and presence in the Ohio Valley.
  • The conflict began in 1754 when the British tried to remove the French from forts in western Pennsylvania.
  • Competition over western land and the fur trade therefore helped trigger the Seven Years' War.

Consequences of the Seven Years' War#

  • Britain defeated France and became the dominant European power in North America.
  • France's defeat removed an important rival that had helped limit British colonial expansion.
  • Native Americans feared that British settlers would now take even more of their land.
  • In 1763, Native groups in the Ohio Valley and Great Lakes launched Pontiac's Rebellion against British rule.
  • Britain responded with the Proclamation of 1763.
  • The Proclamation prohibited colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains and reserved that land for Native Americans.
  • Many colonists and land speculators were angry because they expected to gain western land after the war.
  • The war and its aftermath increased tensions among Britain, the colonists, and Native Americans.
  • These new tensions helped weaken the relationship between Britain and its American colonies.
