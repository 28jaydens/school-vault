AP US History — LEQ Guide

← Exams & Reviews · SAQ Guide · Class Home

What to expect in May 2027

  • One required long essay, recommended time: 40 minutes.
  • Worth 15% of the AP exam score.
  • The prompt gives a broad historical task and a brief introduction. You build an argument using accurate evidence from memory.
  • The reasoning process will center on causation, comparison, or continuity and change over time (CCOT).
  • The LEQ is scored on a 6-point rubric.

The 6 points

Category Points How to earn it
Thesis/claim 1 Make a historically defensible claim that answers the prompt and establishes a line of reasoning. Put it in one place in the introduction or conclusion.
Contextualization 1 Explain a broader development occurring before, during, or after the prompt's period that is relevant to the argument. Do more than name it.
Evidence 1 Describe at least two specific, relevant historical examples.
Evidence supports argument 1 Use at least two specific examples to support the thesis, explaining how each proves the argument.
Historical reasoning 1 Organize the argument using the prompt's reasoning process: causation, comparison, or CCOT.
Complexity 1 Demonstrate a complex understanding through qualification, multiple variables, both continuity and change, similarity and difference, cause and effect, or meaningful connections across periods.

[!tip] Secure the first five points before chasing complexity.
A clear thesis, real context, two explained examples, and consistent reasoning create a strong essay. Complexity should grow naturally from the argument.

A reliable 40-minute plan

  1. Read and mark the task — 2 minutes
    - Circle the reasoning word: causes, effects, compare, evaluate, changed, continued.
    - Underline the subject and dates.
  2. Plan — 5 minutes
    - Write a tentative answer.
    - Select 2–4 specific pieces of evidence.
    - Group evidence into two or three reasons/categories.
    - Identify relevant background for context.
  3. Write — 30 minutes
    - Introduction: context + thesis.
    - Body paragraphs: claim + evidence + explanation.
    - Add qualification or a second perspective where it fits.
  4. Check — 3 minutes
    - Did every paragraph answer the prompt?
    - Did you explain how evidence supports the thesis?
    - Are the evidence and dates historically accurate?

Thesis formula

Although [qualification or counterpoint], because [reason 1] and [reason 2], [main answer to the prompt].

The formula is optional; the thesis must do three things:

  1. Answer the exact question.
  2. Take a defensible position rather than restating the prompt.
  3. Preview the reasoning used in the essay.

Weak thesis

European colonization affected Native Americans in many ways.

This only restates the topic and gives no defensible position or reasoning.

Strong thesis

Although French and Dutch traders often relied on cooperative Native alliances, European colonization from 1492 to 1754 harmed Native sovereignty overall because epidemic disease caused demographic collapse and expanding agricultural settlements produced warfare and displacement.

This answers the question, qualifies the argument, and previews two categories of evidence.

Contextualization formula

Write 2–4 sentences that move from a broader development into the prompt:

Before [prompt development], [broader historical situation]. This mattered because [explain connection]. As a result, [transition to thesis].

Unit 1 example

Before sustained European contact, Native peoples had developed diverse societies adapted to environments across the Americas, from Pueblo irrigation communities to the Haudenosaunee Confederacy. Meanwhile, European monarchies competed for Asian trade, wealth, converts, and imperial power as maritime technology improved. These conditions shaped the different forms of colonization that followed Columbus's 1492 voyage.

Context cannot be a disconnected fact. Explain why it sets up the argument.

Body-paragraph structure

Use C-E-E-L:

  • Claim: Make a paragraph-level argument supporting the thesis.
  • Evidence: Describe a specific event, person, policy, place, or development.
  • Explanation: Explain how the evidence proves the claim and answers the prompt.
  • Link: Connect the paragraph back to the thesis or reasoning process.

Example

European demand for profitable crops intensified conflicts over land. Tobacco made Jamestown economically successful, but growing tobacco exhausted soil and encouraged English settlers to expand into Powhatan territory. This expansion helped provoke Opechancanough's uprising in 1622 and the violence that followed. Therefore, commercial agriculture changed an initially interdependent relationship into a struggle over land and sovereignty.

The scoring comes from the explanation, not merely from mentioning tobacco or Opechancanough.

Match the reasoning process

Causation

  • Separate long-term conditions from immediate causes.
  • Rank causes by importance when the prompt says “evaluate the extent.”
  • Distinguish intended effects from unintended effects.
  • Useful language: because, contributed to, intensified, triggered, consequently.

Comparison

  • Compare the same categories for both subjects.
  • Address at least one meaningful similarity and/or difference as required by the prompt.
  • Explain why the similarity or difference existed.
  • Useful language: similarly, unlike, whereas, both, because.

Continuity and change over time

  • Establish what conditions were like near the beginning.
  • Identify specific changes and continuities.
  • Explain why change occurred or why a continuity persisted.
  • Useful language: continued, increasingly, remained, shifted, by contrast, turning point.

Ways to demonstrate complexity

  • Qualify the thesis with an important exception.
  • Explain both the immediate and long-term effects of a development.
  • Show how two causes interacted rather than listing them separately.
  • Compare different regions, groups, or periods within the argument.
  • Explain both continuity and change even if the prompt emphasizes only one.
  • Connect the topic to a relevant development in another period.

Do not add a random counterargument sentence. The qualification must be supported and woven into the essay.

Final checklist

  • I answered every part of the prompt and stayed within its dates.
  • My thesis makes a defensible claim and previews my reasoning.
  • My contextualization explains relevant broader history.
  • I used at least two specific pieces of accurate evidence.
  • I explained how each major example supports my argument.
  • My organization follows causation, comparison, or CCOT.
  • I added a meaningful qualification or deeper connection when possible.
